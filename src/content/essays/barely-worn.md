---
title: "Barely Worn"
order: 2
---

I’m drowning in stuff. Flotsam and jetsam everywhere, collected over the years, clinging for dear life to shelves, nooks, under the bed, stacked up in withered pages. Bank statements from 3 years ago. A birthday card I can’t bear to part with. An uncomfortable pair of boots that I bought during a moment of creative bravery that I can’t pull off or be bothered to sell. Each thing living a dusty, untouched life in my home, living there because at one moment in time, I let them reside here, their use sidelined for an inevitable future purpose. Of course, I’ll need this unfashionable raincoat, I told myself in 2019. Surely this extra box of the Yasmin contraceptive pill will come into use one day, I said, as I stuffed it into the bottom of my medicine drawer.

I want to scream. There is stuff everywhere, and every time I visit a friend’s house that’s impeccably tidy, considered and curated, I tell myself that I must clean up my stuff. It is imperative. I will start today.

I begin with the clothes.

As a trend-conscious teenager who had a taste for Zara, Topshop (RIP) and other high street gems of the 2000s, stores that churned out scratchy cardigans and polyester dresses, I have a lot of the stuff. I take photos – front, back, labels, that weird stain you can’t get out that you’re certain is turmeric – and I upload to Vinted. The clothes lie in a shimmering, colourful pile on my bed as I repeat the cycle for each piece of clothing, flinging them onto the ever-growing mountain of poor fashion choices with reckless abandon.

The messages start. People haggle, demanding 30 euros off a 50 euro jacket, asking for more photos, decrying the delivery options, and asking me if I’d swap for something of theirs. Surely, I think as I package up a never-worn silk shirt, this has to be how merchants in pulsating souks and markets across the world feel as they haggle and sell their goods. The thrill of the €7 sale radiates through my veins as I make the 20-minute journey to deliver my package to a shop that sells auto manufacturing parts on my lunch break. I feel like a travelling saleswoman pawning off my wares. I half imagine myself on a commune in the future, bartering nuts and berries for metal scraps and wood to build…something?

But when I get to the shop, excited to deliver my pre-loved garment to its new, exciting life, I’m greeted by a sign that declares simply:

“NO VINTED”

I stand in the rain, defeated, clutching my binbag-wrapped package. Trudging home, I promise myself I’ll go tomorrow and try again. It won’t end up on my dresser or squeezed into a drawer for the next two years, I reassure myself, its purpose cemented, destined for a date far into the future.
